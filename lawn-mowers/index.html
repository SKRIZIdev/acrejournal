MOWING TIME IS DECK WIDTH TIMES SPEED

Adjacent demand

Zero-turns, stand-ons and the compact tractor with a deck — how to pick by ground rather than by acreage, and why the advertised dollar figure is often a payment.

Show my match

Mowing is the job that gets bought for last and used first. Most people on a few acres end up with two machines eventually — something that cuts lawn properly and something that handles the rough — and the mistake is buying one machine and expecting it to do both.

Which type of machine

Four options, and the ground picks between them more than the acreage does.

Zero-turn. Fast on open grass with obstacles: trees, beds, posts. The turning circle is the whole point, and it saves real time on a property with things to go round. The weakness is slopes — the front casters unload going downhill and you lose steering with them. Manufacturers publish slope limits and they are not conservative marketing.

Stand-on. Shorter wheelbase, easier to get on and off, better in tight spaces. Popular with contractors who dismount constantly; on a private property the advantage is smaller.

Lawn tractor. Slower, cheaper, more forgiving on slopes, and it can tow. If the ground rolls, this is often the honest answer.

Compact tractor with a mid-mount deck. Only sensible when the tractor is already there. It cuts acceptably and does everything else too, but a dedicated mower will finish the same lawn faster and neater.

What deck width

Mowing time is close to area divided by deck width times ground speed. Width is the lever with the biggest effect, and it is why going from 48 to 60 inches changes an afternoon into an hour and a half.

The constraint is not budget but geometry: gates, gaps between buildings, the space between the last tree and the fence. A deck that cannot pass through your narrowest regular gap will cost you more time in workarounds than it saves in passes.

Engine size is a separate question and it decides behavior rather than speed — what happens when the grass is tall, wet, or both. Underpowered machines do not cut slower, they clog.

What it costs

A warning specific to this category before any numbers.

The dollar figure next to a mower is frequently a monthly finance payment. We pulled Bad Boy's current model range and every published figure was a payment, not a price — a diesel Renegade showing at $384.81 is obviously not a machine price. Comparison sites repeat these numbers as if they were prices, and the result is a model ranking that has nothing to do with cost.

Ask any dealer for an out-the-door price including deck, delivery and setup, and compare those. Below is the current range for orientation, without invented prices.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Zero-turn or lawn tractor?

Zero-turns win on open ground with obstacles to steer around — that is what the turning circle buys you. Lawn tractors win on slopes, because a zero-turn loses steering authority when the caster wheels unload going downhill. If a meaningful part of your ground is sloped, the faster machine is the wrong machine.

Can I just put a deck on my compact tractor?

You can, and it makes sense if the tractor is already there and mowing is occasional. It is slower than a dedicated mower and harder on your patience — a mid-mount deck on a compact is a compromise in both directions. Where it pays is rough grass and brush, which a finishing deck will not touch.

How much deck width do I need?

Mowing time is roughly area divided by width times speed, so width is the single biggest lever on how long the job takes. The limit is your gates and your obstacles — a 60-inch deck that cannot get between the barn and the fence is worse than a 48.

Is a diesel mower worth it?

Only on hours. Diesel earns back its premium through fuel efficiency and engine life at commercial usage. On a few hundred hours a year the saving never covers the extra purchase cost.

Why can I never find a price?

Because in this category the dollar figure shown next to a model is often a monthly finance payment, not the machine's price. Bad Boy's own model pages do this. Always check whether the number you are comparing is a price or a payment before you conclude one machine is cheaper.
