WILL IT RUN ON MY PTO?

Interactive

Enter the PTO horsepower and hitch category your tractor actually has, and see which implements it runs comfortably, which it runs with no margin, and which it cannot.

The number on the hood is engine horsepower. The number an implement cares about is what reaches the PTO shaft after the drivetrain takes its share — always the lower of the two, and the one printed in the implement manual.

How to read the verdicts

Runs it means the tractor is at or above the comfortable rating: the implement works at full width in normal conditions without pulling the engine down.

Tight means you are between the minimum and the comfortable figure. The implement will turn, but in wet grass, heavy soil or deep snow you will be dropping engine speed and taking narrower bites. Plenty of people work like this on purpose — just know that is the deal.

Too much means either the power is short by a real margin, or the hitch category does not match. Category mismatch is the harder of the two: pin sizes and link spacing differ, and an adapter does not add lift capacity.

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Why does the tool ask for PTO horsepower instead of engine horsepower?

Because that is the power the implement actually receives. The gap between the two is typically noticeable, and sizing an implement against the engine number is the most common way people end up with a machine that stalls in wet grass.

My tractor is Category 1 but the implement says Category 2. Can I adapt it?

Adapter bushings exist for pins, but they do not change the lift capacity or the geometry. If the implement is heavier than the hitch was designed to lift, the adapter solves the wrong problem.

What if my implement is not listed?

The list covers the common sizes behind sub-compact through utility tractors. Match yours to the nearest working width and treat the horsepower band as the answer.
