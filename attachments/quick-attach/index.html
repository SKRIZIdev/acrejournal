What is a quick attach, and what does it cost you?

Updated 17 Aug 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer

A quick attach is a mounting plate that lets you change an implement without tools. On a loader it swaps buckets and forks in under a minute and costs you some lift capacity — on a Kubota BX1880 that is 739 lb rigid against 613 lb with the quick attach. On the three-point hitch, a quick hitch does the same job for rear implements.

A quick attach is the plate that turns a twenty-minute job into a one-minute one. It sits between the loader arms and whatever is on the end of them, and it releases with two levers instead of four pins, a hammer and some swearing.

It also costs you lift capacity, and manufacturers are quieter about that than about the convenience.

Front and rear are different things

Two devices share the name in conversation.

Loader quick attach is at the front. It carries buckets, pallet forks, grapples, snow pushers. The pattern matters: Skid Steer (also sold as "universal") is shared across a lot of equipment, while manufacturer-specific patterns are not, and an implement bought for the wrong pattern is an expensive paperweight.

Three-point quick hitch is at the rear. It is a frame that stays on the linkage so you can back into an implement, lift, and drive off, rather than getting off the tractor to line up lower links by hand.

What the loader plate costs in capacity

Kubota publishes both numbers for the BX1880, which is the one model in the BX series where the full specification column is public: 739 lb with a rigid mount, 613 lb with the quick attach.

That is 126 lb, or 17% of the rated capacity, given up for the convenience of not carrying pins in a pocket.

Two mechanisms are at work. The plate is itself steel that the loader has to lift, and it moves the load a little further out from the pivot, which reduces what the same hydraulic force can hold. Neither is a defect; both are geometry.

Most brochures quote whichever figure is larger. If you are sizing work against the loader — lifting pallets of anything, moving heavy round bales — find out which number your machine's figure refers to before you commit.

Why it is usually still worth it

Because the frequency is not symmetric. Changing implements happens constantly; working within the top 17% of rated loader capacity should be rare, and doing it routinely is how loader arms get bent.

The exception is the machine already at its ceiling. If your loader work is at the limit of what the tractor can do, the rigid mount is the honest configuration, and the extra minutes are the price.

What the rear quick hitch costs

The same trade, in a place where it hurts more. Rear lift capacity is quoted at a set distance behind the hitch — 24 inches is the standard we use for every machine on this site — and a quick hitch adds both weight and distance. On a tractor with 681 lb of rated lift, that matters; on one with 1,389 lb, it does not.

It also lifts ground-engaging implements slightly higher, which changes the angle a box blade or a subsoiler works at. Adjustable top links exist for exactly this reason.

Before you buy either one

Check the pattern, not the brand. Check the category — a Category II quick hitch will not fit Category I pins. And check what the implement weighs against the hitch and PTO limits of your own machine, because the quick hitch changes the arithmetic.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

What is a quick attach on a tractor?

A standardised plate between the loader arms and the implement. Two levers or two pins release it, so a bucket comes off and pallet forks go on without spanners and without lining up loose pins by hand. Most manufacturers use their own pattern; Skid Steer (universal) is the most widely shared one.

Does a quick attach reduce lift capacity?

Yes, and manufacturers publish both figures when you look for them. Kubota rates the BX1880 loader at 739 lb rigid and 613 lb with the quick attach — a 126 lb, or 17%, reduction. The plate adds weight and moves the load further from the pivot, and both work against you.

Is a quick attach worth losing capacity for?

For most owners, yes. Swapping implements is something you do dozens of times a season; lifting within the last 17% of rated capacity is something you should not be doing routinely anyway. The exception is a machine already at its limit for the work — there, the rigid mount is the honest choice.

What is the difference between a quick attach and a quick hitch?

Quick attach is at the front, on the loader. A quick hitch is at the rear, on the three-point linkage — a frame that stays on the tractor so implements can be picked up by backing into them instead of crawling underneath with the pins. Same idea, opposite end of the machine.

Do I need a quick hitch on the three-point?

If you swap rear implements more than a few times a season, it pays for itself in knuckles. Two cautions — it raises the implement slightly, which changes the geometry on ground-engaging tools, and it adds weight at the worst possible place for lift capacity — behind the hitch, where your rated figure is measured.
