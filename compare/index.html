Show my match

Every comparison on this site opens with the verdict. Not because the reasoning does not matter, but because a reader who has already chosen between two machines should not have to mine nine paragraphs for the answer.

How a comparison is structured

Four parts, in this order.

The trade-off, in the first paragraph. What you give up by choosing each one. If there is no trade-off, the two machines were not worth comparing.

A specification table with sources. Manufacturer figures, dated, with a link to where they came from. Where we could not read a figure from the manufacturer ourselves, it is marked as unverified rather than quietly copied from someone else's transcription.

The specifications that actually differ in use. Hydraulic flow, lift capacity and PTO power decide daily experience far more than engine horsepower, and they are the numbers buyers skim.

A verdict split by use case, not a score. We do not rank machines out of ten — a single number hides the trade-off, which is the only thing a comparison is for.

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

How do you choose what to compare?

By what people actually cross-shop, which usually means the same class rather than the same price. Two machines are worth comparing when the specification sheets look similar and the ownership experience does not.

Do manufacturers pay for placement here?

No. Nobody pays to appear in a comparison or to be recommended in one. Where a link to a seller could earn us a commission it is marked as sponsored, and the disclosure sits next to the block rather than in the footer.

Why do some comparisons say a figure is unverified?

Because we could not read it from the manufacturer ourselves. Several makers block automated access to their specification pages, and rather than present someone else's transcription as fact we mark it and say so on the page.
