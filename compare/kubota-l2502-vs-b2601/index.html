Kubota L2502 vs B2601

Updated 17 Aug 2026 · 7 min read
Short answer

The B2601 lists at $20,017 and the larger L2502 at $17,449, while their hitch capacities are 22 lb apart. The B2601 buys you a smaller footprint, a mid PTO for a mower deck and 3.1 GPM more total flow. If neither of those matters, the L2502 is the same capability for $2,568 less.

These two get cross-shopped constantly, and the price tag makes no sense at first glance: the bigger machine is cheaper. An L2502 lists at $17,449 and a B2601 at $20,017, and their hitch capacities are 22 lb apart.

So the comparison is not about capability at the hitch. It is about footprint, the mid PTO, and how the hydraulics are counted.

The hydraulic figures are not comparable, and here is why

This is the trap in the table above. Kubota publishes 5.2 GPM for the L2502 as implement flow — what is available to a rear cylinder or a loader. TractorData publishes 8.3 GPM for the B2601 as total flow, which includes the steering circuit.

Total flow is always the larger number. Comparing one machine's total against another's implement flow makes the first look far better than it is, and that comparison appears all over the internet for these two tractors.

What we can say honestly: the B2601's pump moves more oil in total, and its implement flow is not published separately, so the size of its real advantage is unknown. We have not found a source for it, and we are not going to estimate one.

What the extra $2,568 buys

A smaller footprint. The B2601 is a sub-compact. If the machine has to fit a single garage bay, get between raised beds, or turn inside a yard, this is the reason to pay more, and no specification line captures it.

A mid PTO. 2,500 rpm at the belly, which is where a mid-mount mower deck gets its drive. The L2502 has nothing there. For anybody whose list includes mowing a lawn rather than cutting a field, this single line settles the argument.

Three-range hydrostatic as standard, where the L2502 makes you choose between gears and HST — and gives up 1.4 hp at the PTO if you take the hydrostatic.

What the L2502 keeps

More machine for less money. The most PTO power of the pair in gear-drive form, at 19.8 hp. And the fact that it is physically bigger and heavier, which matters for loader stability and for pulling ground-engaging implements in a straight line.

It is also the machine with a full manufacturer specification column, which is not nothing: every figure on its page comes from Kubota, while half the B2601's comes from a reference site.

Which one to buy

Mowing a lawn with a mid-mount deck, or a tight yard — B2601, and the $2,568 is buying something real.

Loader work, land work, a rear cutter or a tiller, with room to drive — L2502, gear drive if implements are the point, and put the difference towards the implement.

What we could not verify

The B2601's implement-circuit flow, its loader lift capacity (the 948 lb figure widely quoted belongs to the B2301 spec sheet), and the L2502's weight, which Kubota does not publish on the series page. Each is left blank above rather than filled with an estimate.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Why is the smaller B2601 more expensive?

Because MSRP describes a base configuration, not a size. Kubota's own lineup is not monotonic — the B2301 lists above the B2401 with less power, the L4802 below the L3902 — and each price belongs to whatever that model ships with as standard. The B2601 is a sub-compact built for work, and it is priced accordingly.

Which one lifts more?

The L2502, but only just — 1,389 lb against 1,411 lb puts the B2601 ahead by 22 lb at 24 inches behind the hitch. Treat them as equal on the hitch and decide on something else.

Which one for a mower deck?

The B2601. It has a mid PTO at 2,500 rpm, which is what a mid-mount deck is driven from; the L2502 has no equivalent. If mowing is on the list at all, this is the whole decision.

Which one for loader work?

The B2601 moves 8.3 GPM total against the L2502's 5.2 GPM implement flow, so the loader cycles quicker. But the L2502 is the bigger, heavier machine, and mass is what holds a loaded bucket down. Fast cycles on the B2601, more stability on the L2502.

Do both avoid the emissions aftertreatment?

Both sit under the threshold as their manufacturer rates them — 23.3 hp and 24.3 hp against a limit of 19 kW, which is 25.5 hp. Note that TractorData rates the B2601 at 25.5 hp gross, right on the line; the figures differ because measurement standards do. Neither machine carries a diesel particulate filter to service.
