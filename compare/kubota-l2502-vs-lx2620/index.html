Kubota L2502 vs LX2620

Updated 16 Aug 2026 · 7 min read
Short answer

Identical engines, different machines. The LX2620 lifts 1,676 lb against 1,389 and moves 8.7 GPM against 5.2, for $2,888 more. If your implement list is growing, take the LX; if it is a loader and a cutter, the L2502 does the same work for less.

These two are the most common cross-shop in Kubota's compact range, and the spec sheets make them look almost identical. Same 23.3 hp engine, same Category I hitch, same class. The gap at the till is $2,888.

The engine is not what separates them.

What the flow difference actually does

Hydraulic flow is the specification buyers skim and owners notice every single day.

At 5.2 GPM the L2502's loader raises, curls and dumps at a perfectly workable speed. At 8.7 GPM the LX2620 does the same cycle around two-thirds faster. Over an afternoon of moving gravel that is not a luxury — it is the difference between forty loads and twenty-five.

It matters more for anything with a hydraulic motor: a grapple worked hard, a hydraulic angle blade, a post driver. Those attachments consume flow continuously, and on 5.2 GPM they work but they work slowly.

What the lift difference does

287 lb of extra capacity at the hitch sounds marginal and is not, because implement weight does not scale gently. A six-foot rotary cutter, a box blade with scarifiers, a heavy tiller — these cluster right around the point where 1,389 lb becomes the constraint.

There is a second-order effect too. Rated lift is measured 24 inches behind the hitch point, and capacity falls as the load moves further back. An implement whose mass sits well behind the pins eats into the margin faster than its weight suggests. With 1,676 lb you have room for that; with 1,389 lb you sometimes do not.

The transmission wrinkle

Here is the detail that flips the comparison for some buyers.

The L2502 in gear form delivers the most PTO power of the three configurations — 19.8 hp. But in hydrostatic form it delivers the least, 18.4 hp, which is below the LX2620's 19.4.

So if you want hydrostatic — and most people buying for loader work do — the LX2620 gives you more power at the PTO and more flow and more lift. The L2502 only wins the power comparison in the gear version, which is the version loader-focused buyers do not want.

Which one to buy

Take the L2502 if the job list is a loader, a cutter and a blade, you are content with gears or do not mind the hydrostatic's 18.4 hp, and the $2,888 is better spent on implements than on the tractor.

Take the LX2620 if the implement list is growing, if anything on it is hydraulically driven, or if you want hydrostatic without giving up PTO power. It is also the one to buy if a cab is ever in the plan, since the L2502 does not offer one.

Neither is the wrong machine. But buying the L2502 and then wanting a grapple is a common and expensive sequence, and it is worth deciding which side of that line you are on before the order is signed.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

They have the same engine — why the price difference?

Because the engine is not what you are paying for. Both are 23.3 hp, but the LX2620 has a stronger three-point hitch (1,676 lb against 1,389), considerably more hydraulic flow (8.7 GPM against 5.2) and an operator platform available with a cab. The frame and the pump are the difference.

Which one for loader work?

The LX2620, for the hydraulic flow rather than the lift. At 8.7 GPM the loader cycles noticeably faster than at 5.2, and over a day of moving material that is the difference you feel. Both handle the weight.

Which one is cheaper to keep?

Both sit under the 25 hp emissions threshold, so neither carries the aftertreatment hardware that starts at 33 hp in this family. Running costs are close; the L2502 is cheaper to buy by $2,888.

Does the L2502 have a hydrostatic option?

Yes. It offers 8F/8R gear at 19.8 PTO hp or three-range hydrostatic at 18.4. The LX2620 is three-range hydrostatic with 19.4 hp at the PTO — so the LX gives you more PTO power than the L2502's own hydrostatic version.
