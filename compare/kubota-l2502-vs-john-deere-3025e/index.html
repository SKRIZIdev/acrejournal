Kubota L2502 vs John Deere 3025E

Updated 16 Aug 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer

Nearly identical engines — 23.3 hp against 24.4 — and a real difference where it counts: 19.8 hp at the PTO on the L2502 against 17.4 on the 3025E. The Deere answers with slightly more hydraulic flow, 5.3 GPM against 5.2. Kubota figures come from the manufacturer; Deere's from TractorData, because deere.com blocks our checks.

The number that decides it

The 3025E has the bigger engine — 24.4 hp against 23.3 — and delivers less power to the implement: 17.4 hp at the PTO against the L2502's 19.8 with the gear transmission.

That is a 2.4 hp gap in Kubota's favour from a smaller engine, and it is the gap that matters, because implements are sized against PTO power. A five-foot rotary cutter that sits comfortably behind the Kubota is marginal behind the Deere.

The comparison narrows if you want hydrostatic: the L2502's HST version drops to 18.4 hp, which is still ahead but by one horsepower rather than two and a half.

Where the Deere answers

Hydraulic flow, marginally: 5.3 GPM against 5.2. In practice that difference is invisible — both machines are on the low side for continuously driven attachments, and if hydraulics matter to you, neither of these is the right answer. Kubota's own LX2620 moves 8.7 GPM and a Kioti CK2620 moves 11.73.

The Deere's stronger arguments are not on the spec sheet at all: dealer density across most of the US, resale, and the loader package your dealer will actually quote.

Price, honestly

The two list prices are not directly comparable. The Kubota figure is current MSRP for the 2026 model year. The Deere figures are from TractorData's price history — $17,324 at the 3025E's 2017 launch and $18,330 in 2020 — because Deere does not publish a current list price we can read.

What that history does show is drift: the same machine gained about $1,000 of list in three years. Expect today's number to be meaningfully higher than either figure, and expect the delivered price with a loader to be higher again.

Which one to buy

The L2502 if implements are the point. More power where the implement takes it, current published pricing, and a manufacturer that publishes its specifications openly.

The 3025E if your nearest dealer is a Deere dealer. That is not a soft argument — parts availability and warranty work over ten years of ownership outweigh 2.4 horsepower.

Neither if hydraulically driven attachments are on your list. Both are around 5 GPM, and the class has much better answers at similar money.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Why do the two columns come from different sources?

Because Kubota publishes its specifications where we can read them and John Deere blocks automated access to its site. Rather than write something to get around that block, we use TractorData — a public industry reference — and label it. It is a secondary source, and the page says so instead of implying we read it from Deere.

Is the L2501 the same as the L2502?

No. The L2501 is the previous generation and has been replaced by the L2502. Listings and older reviews mix the two, and parts are not interchangeable across the board. If you are reading a comparison that still says L2501, check what it is actually about.

Which has the better dealer network?

John Deere, almost everywhere in the US, and it is a real argument rather than a brand one — parts availability and warranty work depend on how far away the dealer is. Kubota's network is strong but thinner in some regions. Check both within your driving distance before comparing specifications at all.

Do both avoid the emissions aftertreatment?

Both are built to sit under the 25 hp (19 kW) EPA threshold, below which the particulate limit is thirteen times looser. That is the reason two machines from different manufacturers land within a horsepower of each other.
