Changing fast

Show my match

Parts is the section where this site currently does least, on purpose. There is no catalogue here and there will not be one until we have fitment data we can stand behind.

What we can do is cover the part of this subject that actually changed this summer, and the two questions that decide whether a parts order goes right.

The repair rules changed in July

On 8 July 2026 the FTC, together with the attorneys general of Illinois, Arizona, Michigan, Minnesota and Wisconsin, announced a proposed settlement with Deere & Company. The core of it is simple: Deere must give farmers and independent repair providers the same repair resources it gives its own authorised dealers, including the software capabilities.

Three details matter for anyone maintaining a machine.

It covers diagnostics, not just spanners. The resources at issue are the electronic ones — reading and clearing fault codes, reprogramming components, and getting a machine running again after an emissions-related shutdown. That is precisely the category that used to force a dealer visit for a fault a competent owner could otherwise clear.

Future tools are covered automatically. Once a new repair tool reaches more than 50% of Deere's authorised dealer locations in the US, it must be made available to owners and independent shops as well. That closes the obvious loophole of simply not releasing new capability outside the dealer network.

It runs for ten years. This is not a gesture with a short shelf life.

What it does not do is make repair simple, or extend to other manufacturers. The order binds Deere. Whether Kubota, Kioti and the rest match it voluntarily is the question worth watching over the next couple of seasons.

How to check fitment

By serial number. Always.

Model names are marketing; serial ranges are engineering. Manufacturers change suppliers and revisions during a production run, and the components most likely to differ are exactly the routine ones — filters, seals, belts, hydraulic fittings. Two tractors with the same badge and two years between them can take different parts.

The serial plate is usually on the frame near the operator platform, with a second number on the engine block. Take a photograph of both and keep it in your phone; every parts counter and every online catalogue will ask.

This is also the reason there is no catalogue on this page. Fitment data lives inside manufacturer systems, and the aggregators that license it expose it only through their own search. We are not going to guess, because the cost of a wrong guess falls on your engine rather than on us.

OEM or aftermarket

Sort this question by what happens when the part fails, not by price.

Where aftermarket is usually fine: air filters, fuel filters, belts, blades, lights, seat parts. If a cheaper item fails you replace it again, and the damage stops there.

Where we would not economise: anything inside the hydraulic or transmission circuit. A filter that bypasses at the wrong pressure, or media that breaks down and circulates, contaminates a system whose repair is measured in thousands. The same logic applies to seals in the loader circuit.

Where "OEM" is not what you think: many original parts are made by the same suppliers who sell into the aftermarket. The question is whether the specification matches, not whose logo is on the box. Match the specification, not the packaging.

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Am I allowed to repair my own tractor now?

For John Deere equipment in the US, the position changed on 8 July 2026. The FTC and five state attorneys general secured a proposed settlement requiring Deere to give farmers and independent repair providers the same repair resources it gives authorised dealers, including software capabilities. The order runs for ten years. It does not make every repair easy, but it removes the argument that only a dealer may hold the tools.

How do I check whether a part fits?

By serial number, not by model name. Manufacturers change suppliers and revisions mid-run, so two tractors wearing the same badge can take different filters, seals or belts. The serial plate is the only reliable key, and any parts counter will ask for it first.

Are aftermarket filters acceptable?

For most service items on a compact tractor, yes — provided the specification matches, not just the dimensions. Where we would not economise is anything inside the hydraulic circuit or the transmission, because the failure mode is contamination of an expensive system rather than simply replacing the part again.

Why is there no parts catalogue on this site?

Because we will not publish fitment data we cannot verify. Compatibility information sits behind manufacturer systems, and aggregators expose it only through their own search. Publishing guesses about what fits would be worse than publishing nothing — a wrong filter is an engine.

Does the Deere settlement help owners of other brands?

Not directly; the order binds Deere. Its practical effect is broader, because it sets an expectation across the industry and gives regulators a template. Whether Kubota, Kioti and the rest follow voluntarily is an open question.
