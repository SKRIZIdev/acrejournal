The B2601 sits in an awkward place in Kubota's own range — above the BX, below the L, and priced above both of the machines it is closest to. The forum record is a useful check on whether that position makes sense to the people who bought one.

How this report was built

We read the 20 discussion threads that TractorByNet displays on the B2601 model page, out of 157 the database reports, and sorted them by what the thread is actually about. We count topics and link to the threads. We do not reproduce post text, we do not publish a rating the source does not have, and we have not driven a B2601 ourselves — this is an account of what owners talk about, not a road test.

What the conversation is about

Cross-shopping is a quarter of it. Five of the twenty threads weigh the B2601 against something else, and the list is instructive: the TYM T25, the John Deere 2025R, and Kubota's own BX2680 — plus two separate threads about a new B2601 against a used one. The comparison set is other sub-compacts at the same money, and the most common rival is the machine one step down in the same showroom.

Loader capacity dominates the attachment talk. The single most specific thread in the sample asks what pallet forks on the LA434 can safely lift, and the rest of that cluster is mowing decks — including belly or finish mower on hills and the practical question of how hard the mid-mount deck is to take off and put back — plus a tiller, a plough, and a pair of rear remotes fitted after purchase.

That mix says something about how these machines are used. Nobody is asking about tillage capacity or ground speed. They are asking how much the loader will pick up and how much of a nuisance the deck is to swap, which is the honest daily life of a sub-compact.

The faults are small and mostly electrical. Five threads deal with something not working: a seat safety switch, three-point lift adjustment, a PTO valve, a PTO lever linkage, and one report of low hydraulic pressure. No engine failures, no transmission failures, no frame or loader mount problems appear in this sample at all.

What we did not find

Worth stating plainly, because absence is evidence too — within these twenty threads there is no discussion of regeneration or emissions equipment, which is what dominates conversation one class up. At 24.3 hp the B2601 sits under the 25 hp threshold, and the subject simply does not come up.

There is also nothing about resale, and nothing about dealer service. Neither absence means the machines are trouble-free; it means the model page skews towards people setting a machine up rather than people getting rid of one.

What this is worth to a buyer

If you are choosing between a B2601 and a BX2680, the forum will not settle it for you, but it tells you the argument is real and common — and our own specification comparison puts the B2601 1,411 lb at the hitch against the BX's 680 lb, which is the difference the threads keep circling.

If you are worried about reliability, this sample is mildly reassuring: two years of recent discussion with no structural failure in it. Check the seat switch works before you hand over money, and take the loader capacity question seriously — it is the one thing owners consistently find out about after the purchase rather than before.

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

How big is the sample behind this report?

Twenty discussion threads out of the 157 the model database reports for the B2601, spanning April 2024 to July 2026. The forum shows only the most recent threads on the model page and we did not read past what it displays. Treat the findings as directional rather than statistical.

Do you reproduce what owners wrote?

No. We count and summarise topics and link to the threads so you can read them at the source. The posts belong to the people who wrote them.

Why is there no star rating here?

The source publishes none for this model. We do not invent a score and we do not import another site's rating into our own markup.

What does the B2601 get cross-shopped against?

On the evidence of these threads — the TYM T25, the John Deere 2025R, and Kubota's own BX2680, plus two separate threads weighing a new B2601 against a used one. Nobody in this sample compared it with a full-size utility tractor.

What goes wrong on a B2601?

Nothing structural appears in this sample. What recurs is small — a seat safety switch, three-point lift adjustment, a PTO valve and a PTO lever linkage, and one report of low hydraulic pressure. That profile is consistent with a machine whose problems are in the switches and linkages rather than the driveline.
