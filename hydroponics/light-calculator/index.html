HOW MUCH LIGHT DOES IT ACTUALLY NEED?

Interactive

How much light your grow actually needs, worked from the daily light integral rather than watts per square foot — and what it costs to run each month.

Lighting advice in this hobby is quoted in watts, and watts are the one number that does not describe light. What the plant responds to is the daily light integral: how much usable light lands on the canopy over the whole day.

How this is worked out

The crop sets a target DLI. Your photoperiod turns that into an intensity — the same daily total can come from a brighter fixture on a short schedule or a dimmer one running longer. Intensity across your growing area gives the total flux that has to leave the fixtures, plus whatever is lost to walls and floor. Divide by what one fixture puts out, round up, and that is the answer.

The wattage falls out at the end. It tells you the bill, not the harvest.

Where people overspend

Two places. The first is buying intensity when hours would do: an extra hour on the timer is free in equipment terms and adds to the daily total exactly like a brighter light does, up to the point where the crop stops using it.

The second is losing light to the room. Going from dark walls to a reflective tent recovers roughly a third of what the fixtures put out — cheaper than another fixture, and it shows up in the calculation above as the utilisation figure.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Why not just use watts per square foot?

Because two fixtures of the same wattage can deliver very different amounts of light. The efficiency figure — micromoles per joule — is what separates them, and it varies by a factor of nearly three across the fixture types in this calculator.

What is DLI and why does it matter more than brightness?

Daily light integral is the total light a plant receives over a day. A bright fixture on a short schedule and a dimmer one running longer can deliver the same DLI. Plants respond to the daily total, so that is what the calculation targets.

The calculator says I need fewer fixtures than the shop recommends. Who is right?

Check what the shop is sizing against. Recommendations built on wattage or on covering a tent corner to corner will always land higher than a calculation aimed at a target DLI.
