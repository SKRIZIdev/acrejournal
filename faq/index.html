What is this site for?

Working out what to buy for one to twenty acres, and what it will actually do once it arrives. The specification sheets in this industry are accurate and misleading at the same time — engine horsepower is not what reaches the implement, a rated lift is measured at a distance you will not use, and the price shown is rarely the price you pay. We publish the numbers that survive contact with the field.

Where do the numbers come from?

Manufacturers, university extension services and public datasets — each with a link and the date we checked it. Prices are read from the seller on the date shown. Where a manufacturer blocks our access, as John Deere does, the page marks the figure unverified rather than restating someone else's transcription.

Do you test equipment yourselves?

No, and we will not write as though we had. Owner reports here are built by counting what owners discuss and linking to the source, with the sample size printed on the page. If we get hands-on with equipment — realistic at greenhouse and hydroponics scale — that page will say exactly what we did.

How do you make money?

Some links to sellers earn a commission, marked as sponsored with the disclosure next to the block rather than buried in the footer. No paid placements, no sponsored articles, no ad networks. Several product notes here tell you to buy the cheaper option or not to buy at all.

How often is it updated?

When the facts move. Model generations change — the Kubota L2501 became the L2502 — prices drift, and regulations shift, as the July 2026 right-to-repair settlement did. Every page shows when it was last touched, so a stale page is visible rather than disguised.

Will this implement run on my PTO?

Three things have to line up. The hitch category has to match — Category I pins are 7/8 in, Category II are 1 1/8 in, and they do not interchange without bushings. The PTO speed has to match, 540 or 1000 rpm. And the implement's power requirement has to be at or below what your tractor delivers at the PTO, which is roughly 15% less than the engine figure on the brochure.

What is the difference between 540 and 1000 rpm?

Shaft speed at the stub. Most implements sized for compact tractors are 540 rpm; 1000 rpm belongs to larger machinery and higher-draw implements. Running a 540 implement at 1000 rpm is a way to destroy it quickly. Some tractors offer both, selected by a lever or by swapping the stub shaft.

My tractor has 25 engine horsepower. What implement width can I run?

Work from the PTO figure, not the engine. A 23.3 hp Kubota L2502 delivers 19.8 hp at the PTO on the gear transmission and 18.4 on hydrostatic. For a rotary cutter that is a five-foot tool in normal conditions; for a tiller, less, because tillage load is continuous rather than intermittent.

Do I need a quick hitch?

Not for one implement. For two or more it pays for itself in the first season — not in money but in whether you actually swap tools instead of leaving one on all year. Check that your implements are quick-hitch compatible before buying the hitch; older tools often need the pin spacing adjusted.

Why is my three-point lift capacity quoted at 24 inches behind the hitch?

Because leverage. The rated figure is measured at a standard distance behind the lift points, and capacity falls as the load moves further back. A heavy implement with its mass well behind the hitch can exceed the effective capacity even when its weight is under the rated number.

What does a box blade do?

It cuts, carries and drops material in one pass. The scarifiers break the surface, the front edge fills the box, and the trailing edge levels what falls out behind. That is the difference from a rear blade, which pushes material to the side and cannot hold any. Gravel drives, rough ground after a build, spreading a load of topsoil — box blade work.

What size box blade for a compact tractor?

Match the width to the tractor's rear track so the blade covers the tyre tracks, then check the weight against your hitch. On a 25 hp class machine that is normally 48 to 60 inches. A 60 inch box blade weighs roughly 400 to 500 lb, which is inside a 1,389 lb hitch and outside a 681 lb one.

Do I need scarifiers?

On anything that has been driven on, yes. Compacted gravel and dry clay will not enter the box without being broken first — without teeth the blade skids across the surface and polishes it. Adjustable or removable teeth are worth having, because on soft material they dig in too much.

Can a box blade be used as ballast?

Yes, and on a sub-compact that is half the reason to own one. A loaded loader lifts the rear of a light tractor; weight behind the axle holds it down. A box blade parked on the hitch is ballast you can also work with, unlike a ballast box full of concrete.

Box blade or land plane?

A land plane rides on skids and floats material into low spots, which makes it better at maintaining an already-good driveway. A box blade cuts, which makes it better at fixing a bad one. If you have potholes and a crown to rebuild, box blade; if you have washboard on a decent surface, land plane.

Why does the tool ask for PTO horsepower instead of engine horsepower?

Because that is the power the implement actually receives. The gap between the two is typically noticeable, and sizing an implement against the engine number is the most common way people end up with a machine that stalls in wet grass.

My tractor is Category 1 but the implement says Category 2. Can I adapt it?

Adapter bushings exist for pins, but they do not change the lift capacity or the geometry. If the implement is heavier than the hitch was designed to lift, the adapter solves the wrong problem.

What if my implement is not listed?

The list covers the common sizes behind sub-compact through utility tractors. Match yours to the nearest working width and treat the horsepower band as the answer.

What is a quick attach on a tractor?

A standardised plate between the loader arms and the implement. Two levers or two pins release it, so a bucket comes off and pallet forks go on without spanners and without lining up loose pins by hand. Most manufacturers use their own pattern; Skid Steer (universal) is the most widely shared one.

Does a quick attach reduce lift capacity?

Yes, and manufacturers publish both figures when you look for them. Kubota rates the BX1880 loader at 739 lb rigid and 613 lb with the quick attach — a 126 lb, or 17%, reduction. The plate adds weight and moves the load further from the pivot, and both work against you.

Is a quick attach worth losing capacity for?

For most owners, yes. Swapping implements is something you do dozens of times a season; lifting within the last 17% of rated capacity is something you should not be doing routinely anyway. The exception is a machine already at its limit for the work — there, the rigid mount is the honest choice.

What is the difference between a quick attach and a quick hitch?

Quick attach is at the front, on the loader. A quick hitch is at the rear, on the three-point linkage — a frame that stays on the tractor so implements can be picked up by backing into them instead of crawling underneath with the pins. Same idea, opposite end of the machine.

Do I need a quick hitch on the three-point?

If you swap rear implements more than a few times a season, it pays for itself in knuckles. Two cautions — it raises the implement slightly, which changes the geometry on ground-engaging tools, and it adds weight at the worst possible place for lift capacity — behind the hitch, where your rated figure is measured.

Compact tractors

Open section
FAQ

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

How many acres do I need before a compact tractor makes sense?

There is no acreage answer, and anyone who gives you one is guessing. A quarter-acre with a gravel drive to plough and a trailer to move needs a loader; twenty acres of standing hay that you pay a neighbor to cut needs nothing. Choose by the heaviest recurring job, not the size of the deed.

Why do so many models stop at 23–25 horsepower?

Because of an emissions threshold, not engineering. US EPA nonroad rules split at 19 kW — 25 hp. Below that line the particulate limit is 0.4 g/kWh; at or above it, 0.03 g/kWh, thirteen times stricter. Manufacturers keep whole model families just under the line, which is why the class clusters at 23.3 and 24.8 hp.

Sub-compact or compact?

Compare the hitch, not the badge. A Kubota BX1880 lifts 680 lb at the three-point; an LX2620 with almost the same 23.3 hp engine lifts 1676 lb and moves 8.7 gallons a minute of hydraulic flow against 6.2. The engines are similar, the machines are not.

Is the manufacturer's price what I will pay?

No, and not only because of tax. MSRP excludes dealer fees, delivery and optional equipment, and it is quoted for the base configuration — usually without the loader that made you want the tractor. Dealers also rarely publish prices at all — the industry sells through a quote, which is why every dealer site has a "build your package" form instead of a cart.

What is the best compact tractor for the money?

There is no single best compact tractor, and any page that names one is selling something. On published figures the value point in the current Kubota range is the L2502 at $17,449 — it undercuts the B2601 sub-compact by $2,568 while lifting more at the hitch. If hydraulics matter, the LX2620 moves 8.7 GPM against 5.2 for $2,888 more, and a Kioti CK2620 moves 11.73. Best for the money depends entirely on which of those numbers your job list actually needs.

What is the best sub compact tractor?

In the Kubota range the BX1880 is the cheapest way in at $12,798 with 13.7 hp at the PTO, and the B2601 is the heavier build at $20,017 with 19.5 hp and a stronger loader. Across brands the John Deere 1025R has the widest dealer network, which matters more over ten years of ownership than a horsepower either way.

Do I need four-wheel drive?

On anything with a slope, mud or a loader, yes. A loaded bucket puts weight ahead of the front axle and takes it off the rear, which is exactly when two-wheel drive stops pulling. Nearly every machine in this class is 4WD as standard for that reason.

How much does a John Deere 1025R cost?

Deere does not publish a list price the way Kubota does, and dealers quote packages rather than machines. The last figure recorded by TractorData for this model is $18,013 for 2026, against $14,301 in 2019 and $13,361 at launch in 2013 — a third more in thirteen years. What you actually pay depends on the loader, the deck and the dealer, and the tractor alone is the smallest part of that number.

What are the 1025R specs that actually matter?

23.8 hp at the engine, 18 hp at the PTO, a Category I hitch that lifts 681 lb at 24 inches behind it, 3.5 GPM of implement flow out of a 6.3 GPM pump, and 1,444 lb of machine. The two figures that decide what it can do are the lift and the flow, not the engine rating.

How much does a 1025R weigh?

1,444 lb bare, on a 57.1 inch wheelbase. That matters more than it sounds — weight is what keeps a loaded loader on the ground, and this is a light machine. Owners running the loader hard add rear ballast rather than argue about horsepower.

What attachments fit a 1025R?

A 54 or 60 inch mid-mount mower deck, a front-end loader, a backhoe, a blade and a snowblower are the factory list. Anything on the three-point hitch has to respect 681 lb and 18 PTO hp — that rules out most rotary tillers and full-size rotary cutters, which is the single most common mismatch in this class.

Is the 1025R being replaced?

TractorData lists production as 2013 to 2026 and shows a 1M series arriving for 2027. A model at the end of its cycle is the one dealers are most willing to move, so it is worth asking what is left on the lot rather than ordering.

1025R or Kubota L2502?

They are not in the same class, even though the engine ratings are two tenths apart. The 1025R lifts 681 lb and moves 3.5 GPM; the L2502 lifts 1,389 lb and moves 5.2 GPM. The Deere is a groundskeeping machine with a mid-mount deck; the Kubota is a working tractor with no deck option. Pick by which of those two jobs is yours.

What is the difference between a 1025R and a 2025R?

The engine and the PTO rating are effectively the same — 24.2 hp against 23.8, and 18 hp at the PTO on both. The difference is the hitch and the mass. The 2025R lifts 1,150 lb at 24 inches behind the hitch against 681 lb, and it weighs 1,660 lb against 1,444. If your implement list is a mower deck and a blade, the extra buys you nothing. What are the 2025R specifications?

24.2 hp engine, 18 hp at the PTO, Category I hitch lifting 1,150 lb at 24 inches (1,433 lb measured at the lift ends), 3.4 GPM of implement flow out of 5.6 GPM total, 1,660 lb of machine on hydrostatic drive, rear PTO at 540 rpm and mid PTO at 2,000.

How much does a 2025R cost?

We do not have a current figure and will not invent one. TractorData's most recent original price for this model is $15,348 for 2014; Deere does not publish a list price to us, and dealers quote a package rather than a machine. Treat the 2014 number as history, not as a target.

Can it run a rotary tiller?

The hitch can hold one, the PTO is the question. 18 hp at the PTO is at the bottom of what a four to five foot tiller is specified to need, and tillers are a continuous load, not a peak one. A narrower tiller worked slowly is realistic; a five-foot tiller in heavy soil is not.

Is a mid-mount mower deck available?

Yes — 54 and 62 inch decks are on the factory list, along with a front-end loader, a backhoe, a blade and a snowblower. The mid PTO runs at 2,000 rpm, which is what the deck is driven from.

What are the John Deere 3025E specifications?

Why does a bigger engine send less power to the PTO than a Kubota L2502?

Because the two figures are measured in different places and lose different amounts on the way. The 3025E is rated 24.4 hp at the engine and 17.4 hp at the PTO — a loss of seven horsepower. Does the 3025E take a mid-mount mower deck?

No. TractorData lists no mid PTO for this model, and a mid-mount deck is driven from one. The factory attachment list is a front-end loader and a backhoe. If mowing a lawn is the main job, this is the wrong machine and a 2025R or a Kubota BX is the right one.

How much does a 3025E cost?

No current list price is published to us. The most recent original price recorded by TractorData is $18,330 for 2020, against $17,324 in 2017. Dealers in this class sell a configured package rather than a machine, so the tractor's own price is only part of the number you will be quoted.

Is it heavy enough for loader work?

At 2,222 lb it is the heaviest machine on our shortlist and considerably heavier than the sub-compacts, which is an advantage here — mass is what holds a loaded bucket down. This is the practical argument for stepping up from a 2025R.

Which John Deere compact tractor should I buy?

Decide on the hitch, not the badge. The 1025R lifts 681 lb and takes a mid-mount deck; the 2025R lifts 1,150 lb on the same engine and PTO rating; the 3025E lifts 1,356 lb and moves half again the hydraulic flow but cannot take a mid-mount mower. If you are mowing, that last point rules the 3025E out before any other comparison starts.

What is the difference between the 1025R and the 2025R?

On paper the engines are near-identical — 23.8 against 24.2 gross horsepower, both with the same 18 hp claimed at the PTO. The 2025R lifts 1,150 lb against 681, weighs 216 lb more, and carries a heavier frame. You are paying for the hitch and the mass, not for engine power.

Why do John Deere compact tractors stop at about 24 horsepower?

The US EPA nonroad rules split at 19 kW, which is 25 hp. Below the line the particulate limit is 0.4 g/kWh; at or above it the limit is 0.03 — thirteen times stricter, and it brings aftertreatment with it. Every manufacturer in this class parks models just under the threshold, which is why the whole segment clusters at 23 to 24.8 hp.

Does John Deere publish prices for these machines?

Not to us. Deere's own site does not serve our checks, and dealers in this industry sell through a quote rather than a listed price. The figures on this page come from TractorData's reference entries and are labelled with the year they belong to — the 1025R's $18,013 is a 2026 figure, the 3025E's $18,330 is from 2020.

How does a John Deere compare with a Kubota of the same size?

On PTO power the Kubota does more with less engine — an L2502 puts 19.8 hp on the shaft from 23.3 gross, while a 3025E puts 17.4 from 24.4. On lift the L2502 does 1,389 lb against the 3025E's 1,356. What Deere brings is dealer coverage, which is a real specification over ten years of ownership.

Is the 1025R's 681 lb hitch enough?

For a box blade, a rotary cutter of the right width and light three-point work, yes. For a tiller, a heavy blade or anything at the top of the Category I range it is the limiting number on the machine, and it is the reason people move up to the 2025R.

Why does the B2601 cost more than the larger L2502?

Because MSRP in this class tracks the base configuration, not the size of the machine. The B2601 lists at $20,017 against $17,449 for an L2502 — $2,568 more for a smaller tractor that lifts 22 lb more at the hitch. What are the B2601 specifications?

Is the B2601 a sub-compact or a compact?

Kubota sells it as a sub-compact and TractorData files it as a compact utility tractor, which tells you where it sits — the frame and footprint are sub-compact, the hitch is not. At 1,411 lb of lift it is within 22 lb of the compact L2502 and more than double a 1025R.

What can it run on the PTO?

19.5 hp at the shaft covers a four-foot rotary cutter, a light rotary tiller worked slowly, a snowblower and a mid-mount deck from the 2,500 rpm mid PTO. It does not cover a five-foot tiller in heavy ground — that is a machine-and-a-half job in this class regardless of brand.

Does the horsepower figure differ between sources?

Yes, and we publish the manufacturer's. Kubota states 24.3 hp; TractorData lists 25.5 hp gross for the same model. Measurement standards differ, so we take the figure from the company that builds it and note the other one rather than quietly picking the bigger number.

Is 13.7 hp at the PTO enough for a mower deck?

How much land does a BX1880 suit?

What is the difference between a BX1880 and a BX2680?

The engine, and almost nothing else that matters structurally. Both sit on the same frame with the same 680 lb hitch and the same 6.2 GPM pump. The BX1880 has 16.6 hp and 13.7 at the PTO; the BX2680 has 23.3 hp and 19.5 at the PTO, for $4,834 more. You are buying power, not capacity.

What are the BX2680 specifications?

Is 680 lb of lift enough?

For a blade, a light box scraper, a snowblower and a mid-mount deck, yes. For a rotary cutter or a tiller, no — most in this size weigh more than the hitch is rated to lift before they touch the ground. Buyers who need ground engagement should be looking at a B2601 or an L2502, which lift roughly twice as much.

Why does a BX2680 have more PTO power than the hitch can use?

Because the two limits are independent. 19.5 hp at the shaft would drive a wider implement than 680 lb of hitch will carry, so on this machine the hitch is the binding constraint. It is the opposite of the John Deere 2025R, where a stronger hitch is limited by 18 PTO hp.

Does it take a loader?

Yes, and it is the usual configuration. Kubota does not publish the loader lift figure for this model — only for the first model of the series — so we do not quote one.

Kubota L2502

Why is this model 23.3 horsepower and not 26?

To stay under the US EPA nonroad threshold at 19 kW, which is 25 hp. Below that line the particulate limit is 0.4 g/kWh; at or above it the limit is 0.03 g/kWh, thirteen times stricter, and the machine needs aftertreatment hardware to meet it. Kubota keeps the whole L02 entry model under the line, which is why the next step up in the family jumps straight to 33 hp.

What does the L2502 replace?

The L2501. The L02 generation superseded the L01 range, and dealers were running clearance offers on L02 stock through the 2026 season. If you are reading a review or a forum thread about an L2501, the specifications are close but not identical, and the parts numbers are not interchangeable across the board.

DT or HST — which transmission?

The gear version (DT, 8 forward and 8 reverse) gives 19.8 hp at the PTO; the hydrostatic gives 18.4. Gear drive is the choice for steady work in a field, where you set a speed and stay there. HST wins wherever you change direction constantly — loader cycles, yard work, snow — at the cost of about 1.4 hp and a slightly higher price.

Is 5.2 GPM of hydraulic flow enough?

For a loader and a rear implement, yes. For anything with a hydraulic motor running continuously — a grapple used hard, a hydraulic-drive attachment — it is on the low side. An LX2620 moves 8.7 GPM for comparison, and that difference is more noticeable in daily use than the horsepower figures suggest.

What does it actually cost to put one in the yard?

The $17,449 list price is the base machine, before tax, dealer fees, delivery and the loader. Dealers in this class rarely publish a delivered price at all — they publish a configurator. Budget well above list once a loader and a mid-mount deck are on it.

How much snow does a tunnel need to carry?

Ultraviolet light and abrasion, in that order. UV-stabilised products yellow and crack more slowly, and most premature failures happen where the film rubs on the frame. How much light do leafy greens need?

Lettuce and most greens do well at a daily light integral of roughly 12–17 mol per square meter per day; fruiting crops such as tomatoes want more than double that. DLI combines intensity and hours, which is why a modest fixture running eighteen hours can beat a powerful one running eight.

Why not just shop by watts?

Because watts measure what you pay the utility, not what the plant receives. Two 400 W fixtures can differ substantially in usable light depending on diodes and driver efficiency. Size by DLI for the crop and the photoperiod, then check that the wattage fits your circuit.

What does a starter setup cost?

A complete 2x2 system with integrated controls starts around $499, and a 4x4 around $999 at current AC Infinity list prices. Buying separately can come in lower, but the saving disappears the first time a fan is mismatched to the tent and you buy twice.

Do I need a controller?

For one tent, not strictly — a timer and a thermometer will do. Controllers earn their place when fans, lights and humidity have to respond to each other, which is where most grows go wrong in week three rather than week one.

How much electricity will it use?

Multiply the fixture wattage by hours per day and divide by 1,000 to get kilowatt hours, then apply your rate. A 200 W light on eighteen hours is 3.6 kWh a day — roughly 108 kWh a month before fans and dehumidification, which typically add a third again.

Irrigation

Open section
FAQ

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Is drip always better than sprinkler?

On water use, yes — drip puts over 90% of applied water into the root zone against 50–70% for overhead sprinklers. On everything else it depends. Drip needs clean water and a filter, it suits row and tree crops with fixed spacing, and it is easy to damage with a hoe. Sprinklers tolerate dirty water, cover irregular plantings and survive cultivation, but lose water to evaporation and drift on a hot windy day.

How long does drip tape last?

One to five seasons, decided mostly by wall thickness and how the tape is handled at the end of the year. Thin-wall tape is a single-season consumable by design. Thicker tape survives being rolled up and put out again, provided the roll does not sit in sunlight over winter. Rodents are a bigger threat than UV in most places.

What pressure does a drip system need?

Most tape runs at 8–12 psi, and mains pressure is far higher than that — which is why a pressure regulator is not optional. The order is filter, then regulator, then tape. Reverse the first two and grit reaches the regulator seat; skip the regulator and the tape splits at the seam.

Can I run drip off a garden hose?

Yes, for a garden-sized block. A hose bib gives enough flow for roughly 100–200 feet of tape at a time, depending on emitter spacing and supply pressure. Beyond that the far end starves and the block should be split into zones on a timer.

How often should I water?

That is a soil question before it is a crop question. Sand holds about a third of the plant-available water that loam does, so the same crop on sand may need water daily and on loam every three or four days. Our calculator reads the mapped soil series from the USDA survey and turns it into an interval.

What is drip tape, and which wall thickness do you need?

Open section
FAQ

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

What is drip tape?

Thin-walled polyethylene tubing with emitters built into the wall at a fixed spacing, usually 8, 12 or 16 inches. Water enters at low pressure and leaves through those emitters at a known rate per hundred feet. It is sold on rolls, laid down the crop row, and replaced when it fails rather than repaired.

What is the difference between drip tape and drip line?

Drip tape is thin-walled and flat when empty, sold by the mil, and treated as semi-disposable. Drip line is thicker-walled round tubing with emitters welded inside, stiffer, more expensive, and intended to stay in place for years. Row crops get tape; perennials, orchards and permanent beds get line.

What wall thickness of drip tape should I buy?

6 mil for a single season on annual vegetables where the tape will be pulled up at the end of the year. 8 to 10 mil where you want two or three seasons and the ground is reasonably clean. 15 mil where there are stones, insects or rodents, or where the tape is left in place over winter. Thicker tape does not water better — it just survives longer.

What emitter spacing do I need?

Match it to the crop spacing and the soil. Close spacing (8 inches) suits carrots, onions and anything sown in a continuous band, and sandy soil where water goes straight down. Wider spacing (12 to 16 inches) suits tomatoes, peppers and squash, and heavier soil where the wetted zone spreads sideways on its own.

How long does drip tape last?

One to five seasons, and the wall thickness plus how it is stored decides which. Sun on empty tape and freezing water inside it are what finish it. Tape drained and rolled up dry lasts materially longer than tape left in the field.

Do I need a pressure regulator for drip tape?

Yes. Tape runs at 8 to 12 psi and mains pressure is several times that — the first thing that happens without a regulator is that the tape splits at the seam. Filter and regulator are the two parts a drip system cannot be assembled without.

Drip or sprinkler for a small holding

Open section
FAQ

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Which uses less water?

Drip, by a wide margin. Application efficiency is over 90% for drip against 50–70% for overhead sprinklers, and Penn State puts the typical comparison at 95% against 70%. On a metered supply or a limited well that difference can decide the whole question.

Which is cheaper to install?

For a small block, drip — tape costs cents per foot and a starter kit runs in the low hundreds. Over a larger irregular area sprinklers usually win, because drip needs a line along every row and sprinklers cover ground indiscriminately.

Can I run both?

Most small holdings end up doing exactly that. Drip in the beds and rows where the spacing is fixed, sprinklers for germination, for irregular ground and for frost protection. They share a filter and a mainline; only the zone hardware differs.

Which survives cultivation?

Sprinklers. Drip tape is destroyed by a hoe or a cultivator without the operator noticing, and a torn line loses pressure for everything downstream of it.

Which handles dirty water?

Sprinklers, easily. Drip emitters have openings measured in thousandths of an inch and clog on anything the water carries. With pond or iron-bearing well water, filtration stops being an accessory and becomes the design constraint.

Types of irrigation, and what each one costs you in water

Open section
FAQ

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

What are the main types of irrigation?

Three families. Surface irrigation moves water across the field by gravity — flood and furrow are the common forms. Sprinkler irrigation throws it through the air — hand lines, wheel lines, center pivots, linear moves and traveling guns. Micro irrigation delivers it at the plant — drip tape, drip line and micro-sprays. Everything sold under a brand name is a variation inside one of those three.

What is furrow irrigation?

A form of surface irrigation where water runs down small parallel channels cut between crop rows and soaks in sideways and down as it advances. The crop sits on the ridge, the water runs in the furrow. It needs a field with a consistent gentle slope, and its efficiency depends almost entirely on getting the stream size and the run length right.

What is flood irrigation?

Surface irrigation where a whole basin or border strip is covered with water rather than being fed down individual furrows. It is the oldest method still in commercial use, the cheapest to install where water is delivered by canal, and the least precise — parts of the field get more than they need so that the far end gets enough.

What is center pivot irrigation?

A sprinkler lateral, several hundred feet long, that rotates around a fixed point in the middle of the field. Standard geometry is a quarter section — half a mile square, 160 acres — with the pivot sweeping a circle inside it. The corners stay dry unless a corner arm or an end gun covers them.

Which type of irrigation is most efficient?

Drip, on paper and usually in practice — extension figures put it at 90% and above, against 80–97% for a well-specified center pivot and 40–90% for surface systems. But the spread inside each method is wider than the gap between methods — a badly managed drip system loses to a well-managed pivot.

Is spray irrigation the same as sprinkler irrigation?

In everyday use, yes — both mean throwing water through the air. Inside the industry "spray" usually points at the nozzle package rather than the machine — mid-elevation spray application on a pivot, as opposed to impact sprinklers above the crop or LEPA heads dropped down near the ground.

What is an irrigation system?

The set of parts that takes water from a source and puts a measured amount of it in the root zone — a pump or a gravity head, a filter, a pressure regulator where the emitters need one, a mainline, and whatever does the applying at the end. The applicator is what people name the system after, but the parts ahead of it decide whether it works.

Does the method decide how much water I use?

Less than the scheduling does. Method sets the ceiling on efficiency; when and how long you run it decides how close you get to that ceiling. The largest single saving on most holdings is not a new system but watering to a measured soil moisture deficit.

Zero-turn or lawn tractor?

Zero-turns win on open ground with obstacles to steer around — that is what the turning circle buys you. Lawn tractors win on slopes, because a zero-turn loses steering authority when the caster wheels unload going downhill. If a meaningful part of your ground is sloped, the faster machine is the wrong machine.

Can I just put a deck on my compact tractor?

You can, and it makes sense if the tractor is already there and mowing is occasional. It is slower than a dedicated mower and harder on your patience — a mid-mount deck on a compact is a compromise in both directions. Where it pays is rough grass and brush, which a finishing deck will not touch.

How much deck width do I need?

Mowing time is roughly area divided by width times speed, so width is the single biggest lever on how long the job takes. The limit is your gates and your obstacles — a 60-inch deck that cannot get between the barn and the fence is worse than a 48.

Is a diesel mower worth it?

Only on hours. Diesel earns back its premium through fuel efficiency and engine life at commercial usage. On a few hundred hours a year the saving never covers the extra purchase cost.

Can you mow wet grass, and what does it actually cost you?

Open section
FAQ

Can you mow wet grass?

Yes, with three consequences. Clippings clump rather than scattering, so they smother the turf under them and have to be raked or spread. The cut is worse, because wet leaves fold away from the blade instead of standing up to it. And traction goes — on a wet slope a ride-on or a zero-turn can slide, which is the part that actually hurts people.

Does mowing wet grass spread lawn disease?

It can move fungal spores around on the deck and tyres, and the conditions that make grass wet are the same ones that let disease establish — extended leaf wetness is what most turf pathogens need. Wet mowing is not the cause; it is one more way to distribute something already present. If a patch is visibly diseased, mow it last and hose the deck afterwards.

Will mowing wet grass damage the mower?

Not the engine, but the deck and the blade take it. Wet clippings pack into the underside of the deck, which chokes the airflow the cut depends on, and they hold moisture against bare metal where paint has worn through. Scrape the deck the same day rather than the next weekend.

Is it better to wait until the grass dries?

When you can, yes. But the one-third rule is the harder constraint — never take off more than a third of the leaf in one pass. If waiting three more days for dry weather means removing half the plant, the wet cut is the smaller injury.

What height should I cut a cool-season lawn?

Between 2½ and 3½ inches, at the taller end for a lawn you do not intend to fuss over. Taller grass shades the soil, roots deeper and needs less water, and it does not magnify a bad wet cut the way a short cut does.

How often should mower blades be sharpened?

Extension guidance is every one to three years depending on how much the machine is used — and sooner if the cut tips look frayed and whitish rather than clean. A dull blade is the single biggest multiplier of wet-grass damage, because a torn leaf loses more water and takes longer to close.

How much water does a crop actually need?

Open section
FAQ

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Why does soil type matter more than the crop?

Because soil sets the size of the reservoir. A crop on sand and the same crop on silt loam use water at a similar rate, but the sand holds around a third as much of it — so the sand plot runs dry three times sooner. Changing crop changes the demand a little; changing soil changes the storage a lot.

How do I find out what my soil is?

The USDA soil survey has mapped essentially the whole country and the data is public. Our drip calculator queries it directly: give it a ZIP code and it returns the mapped soil series and its water holding capacity, then converts that into an interval.

Does rooting depth change the answer?

Substantially. The reservoir is water holding capacity multiplied by the depth roots actually occupy. Shallow-rooted salad crops draw on two or three times less stored water than a deep-rooted crop on the same ground, which is why they need watering more often, not more heavily.

Should I water little and often, or deeply and rarely?

Deep and less often, within what the soil can hold. Light frequent watering wets only the surface and encourages roots to stay there, which makes the crop more vulnerable the first time you miss a day. The exception is genuinely sandy ground, where the reservoir is too small to do anything else.

What does the calculator not know?

Your weather, your wind, your water salinity and what your crop did last week. It models the soil side accurately and the rest generically. Treat it as the starting interval and adjust from what you see in the field.

What is a hydrostatic transmission, and when is gear drive better?

Open section
FAQ

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

How does a hydrostatic transmission work?

The engine drives a variable-displacement hydraulic pump. The pump sends oil to a hydraulic motor connected to the wheels. Tilting the pump's swashplate changes how much oil moves and in which direction, so one pedal gives you infinitely variable speed forwards and reverse with no clutch and no gear to select.

Does a hydrostatic transmission lose power?

Yes. Converting mechanical power to fluid flow and back is not free, and the loss shows up at the PTO. Kubota publishes both figures for the L2502 — 19.8 hp at the PTO with the gear transmission, 18.4 hp with the hydrostatic. That is 1.4 hp, about 7%.

Is hydrostatic or gear drive better for a compact tractor?

It depends on one thing — how often you change direction. Loader work is a cycle of forward, stop, reverse, stop, hundreds of times an hour, and doing that on a clutch is exhausting. Field work with an implement is one direction at one speed for hours, where gears hold the speed without effort and give you the extra horsepower.

Is a hydrostatic transmission reliable?

On modern compacts, yes, with one caveat — the fluid is the transmission. Hydrostatic drives are less tolerant of neglected oil and clogged filters than a gearbox, because the oil is doing the work rather than just lubricating. Service intervals in the manual are not advisory.

Why does hydrostatic feel slower uphill with a load?

Because the drive is pressure-limited rather than geared. As resistance rises, so does system pressure, and the machine slows until the load and the available pressure balance. A geared machine in the same situation either pulls or stalls the engine. Three-range HST exists to give you a lower range for exactly this.

What is a PTO on a tractor?

Open section
FAQ

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

What does PTO stand for on a tractor?

Power take-off. It is the shaft that takes power off the engine and sends it to an implement rather than to the wheels — which is what separates a tractor from a vehicle that merely tows things.

What is PTO on a tractor used for?

Driving anything that needs rotating power rather than pulling: a rotary cutter, a tiller, a post-hole auger, a baler, a snow blower. If an implement has moving parts of its own, it runs off the PTO; if it only needs to be dragged or lifted, it does not.

540 or 1000 rpm — which do I need?

Whatever the implement is built for. 540 rpm is the standard for implements sized to compact and utility tractors; 1000 rpm belongs to larger machinery with higher power draw. At full recommended speed a 540 shaft turns nine times a second and a 1000 shaft about 16.6 times a second.

Can I run a 1000 rpm implement at 540?

No, and the design prevents you from trying by accident: the spline profiles differ, with more splines on the 1000 rpm shaft. An implement engineered for one input speed will not perform at the other, and driving a 540 implement at 1000 rpm is a way to destroy its gearbox quickly.

What are 540E and 1000E?

Economy modes. They deliver the same shaft speed at lower engine rpm, which saves fuel and noise on light work. They are not a separate standard — the implement still sees 540 or 1000 at the shaft.

Why is PTO horsepower lower than engine horsepower?

Because the drivetrain takes its share on the way. A Kubota L2502 with a 23.3 hp engine delivers 19.8 hp at the PTO with the gear transmission and 18.4 with hydrostatic — a loss of roughly 15% and 21% respectively. Implements are sized against the PTO figure, not the engine one.

What is drip irrigation?

What is a drip irrigation system made of?

Five parts in a fixed order: a water source, a filter, a pressure regulator, a mainline, and drip tape or tubing with emitters. The order matters more than the brand — the filter goes ahead of the regulator, and the regulator ahead of any tape. Reverse the first two and grit reaches the regulator seat; skip the regulator and the tape splits at the seam.

What is a drip irrigation system, in one sentence?

A drip irrigation system is a filter, a pressure regulator and a network of tubing with emitters that delivers water at low pressure straight to the root zone — the whole assembly, as opposed to the tape alone.

How does drip irrigation work?

Water is delivered under low pressure — typically 8–12 psi — through tubing laid along the row. Emitters spaced at intervals meter it out a few liters per hour each, so it soaks into the root zone instead of running off or evaporating. The soil, not the pipe, does the distributing.

Is drip irrigation better than sprinklers?

On water use, decisively: over 90% application efficiency against 50–70% overhead, and Penn State puts the typical comparison at 95% against 70%. On everything else it depends — drip needs clean water, suits fixed plant spacing, and is destroyed by a hoe. Sprinklers handle dirty water, irregular plantings and frost protection.

How much does drip irrigation cost?

Hardware for a garden block starts in the low hundreds, and tape itself costs cents per foot. The cost that catches people out is not the first purchase but the replacement cycle: tape lasts one to five seasons depending on wall thickness, while filters, regulators and fittings last years.

What is drip tape?

Thin-walled tubing with emitters built in at set spacing, sold in rolls and laid along the row. It is the consumable part of a drip system — designed to be replaced — as opposed to drip tubing, which is thicker, more expensive and lasts far longer.

Can drip irrigation be used for a lawn?

Poorly. A lawn needs the whole surface wetted evenly, which is exactly what drip does not do — it wets a line of root zones. Subsurface drip lines exist for turf, but for most people a sprinkler is the right tool for grass and drip is the right tool for rows and beds.

Are aftermarket filters acceptable?

For most service items on a compact tractor, yes — provided the specification matches, not just the dimensions. Where we would not economise is anything inside the hydraulic circuit or the transmission, because the failure mode is contamination of an expensive system rather than simply replacing the part again.

Why is there no parts catalogue on this site?

Because we will not publish fitment data we cannot verify. Compatibility information sits behind manufacturer systems, and aggregators expose it only through their own search. Publishing guesses about what fits would be worse than publishing nothing — a wrong filter is an engine.

Does the Deere settlement help owners of other brands?

Not directly; the order binds Deere. Its practical effect is broader, because it sets an expectation across the industry and gives regulators a template. Whether Kubota, Kioti and the rest follow voluntarily is an open question.

How many hours is too many on a compact tractor?

The number alone tells you very little. A machine with 1,200 hours over twelve years has been used gently and maintained on a schedule; the same 1,200 hours over two years means commercial work. On compacts, the more useful signal is hours per year — under about 100 is light homeowner use, and that is the majority of this market.

Is a used tractor with a loader worth the premium?

Usually, because retrofitting one costs more than the price difference and the factory-matched loader fits properly. What to check is whether the loader was matched to that model or adapted, and whether the frame shows repair around the mounts.

Why is the first year of depreciation so steep?

For the same reason it is on a car — the second owner is not paying for delivery, dealer prep or the loader package's retail margin. That is also the argument for buying at one to three years old, where most of the drop has happened and the machine still has a warranty history.

Should I buy from a dealer or a private seller?

A dealer machine costs more and comes with recourse, service records and often a limited warranty. A private machine is cheaper and everything is on you. For a first tractor, where you cannot yet tell a worn hydraulic pump by sound, the dealer premium is cheap insurance.

What about auctions?

Auction prices look attractive until you count what you cannot do — start it under load, drive it, or return it. They suit buyers who can assess a machine cold and absorb a bad one. If that is not you, the saving is not real.

Do hours matter more than age?

Both, but a low-hour older machine usually beats a high-hour newer one on a private lot.

What does dealer-serviced actually buy me?

Documented maintenance, and a much shorter list of surprises in the first season. It is not a warranty, but it is evidence — and the absence of any record on a dealer machine is a question worth asking out loud.
