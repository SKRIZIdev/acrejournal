THE RIGHT SIZE IS SMALLER THAN YOU THINK

Show my match

A compact tractor is bought for one or two jobs and then used for a dozen. That is the whole difficulty of choosing one: the machine that is perfect for mowing five acres is the machine you resent the first time you try to lift a pallet with it.

This section works from the jobs backwards — what the classes really differ on, what the current lineup costs from the manufacturer, and where the specification sheet quietly stops telling you the truth.

Which is the best compact tractor?

The honest answer is that "best" is decided by your heaviest recurring job, not by a ranking — and the specifications below show why. Two machines with the same engine can differ by two and a half times in lift capacity.

What we can say from published figures, checked 16 August 2026:

  • Cheapest way into the class: Kubota BX1880, $12,798, 13.7 hp at the PTO.
  • Best value in a full compact: L2502 at $17,449 — more lift than the sub-compact above it and $2,568 cheaper.
  • Best for hydraulically driven attachments: not either of those. The LX2620 moves 8.7 GPM and a Kioti CK2620 moves 11.73, against 5.2 for the L2502.
  • Best dealer coverage: John Deere, in most of the US, which is a real specification when a machine needs parts in year eight.

Three classes and one confusing middle

Sub-compact, compact, utility. The names suggest a neat ladder of size, and horsepower suggests the same — which is why so many people buy the wrong one.

Look at two Kubotas. The BX1880 is a sub-compact with a 16.6 hp engine and 13.7 hp at the PTO. The LX2620 is a compact with 23.3 hp. So far, an ordinary step up. But the BX lifts 680 lb at the three-point hitch and the LX lifts 1676 lb — two and a half times more — and moves 8.7 gallons per minute of hydraulic flow against 6.2. The class difference is not the engine. It is the frame, the hitch and the pump.

That matters because implements are matched to the hitch and the PTO, not to the badge on the hood. A rotary tiller that needs 1400 lb of lift and 20 PTO horsepower will physically attach to a sub-compact and then fail to do the work.

Why the lineup stops just under 25 horsepower

Look down any manufacturer's compact range and you will notice engines bunched at 23.3 and 24.8 hp, with a jump straight to 33. That is not a coincidence of engineering. It is a regulatory line.

US EPA nonroad standards split the small-engine bands at 19 kW, which is 25 hp. Below that line, the particulate limit is 0.4 g/kWh. At or above it, the limit is 0.03 g/kWh — thirteen times stricter — and NOx plus hydrocarbons tighten from 7.5 to 4.7 g/kWh.

Meeting the stricter band costs money and adds aftertreatment hardware that has to be maintained and eventually replaced. So manufacturers build families that sit just below the threshold. For a buyer this cuts both ways: under 25 hp you get a simpler machine with less to service, and above it you get real capability. What you should not do is pay the higher-band price for a machine you will only ever use at half load.

Two things in that table are worth pausing on.

Price does not follow power. Read the last column top to bottom and it goes up, down and up again. The L4802 has 48.4 hp — the most in the table — and lists $1,256 under the 37.5 hp L3902 and $7,159 under the 39.8 hp LX4020. Inside the B01 range the 20.9 hp B2301 lists $3,644 above the more powerful B2401. These are not typos: we re-checked every row against Kubota's own series pages. "As low as" describes a different base configuration on every model — a bare-bones big machine can list under a well-equipped small one, and a loader, a cab or a hydrostatic transmission moves the number more than a few horsepower do. The practical reading is that a headline price tells you almost nothing until you fix the configuration.

A compact can undercut a sub-compact. The L2502 lists at $17,449 against $20,017 for the B2601, with a heavier hitch and more PTO power. If the jobs point to a compact, the sub-compact is not automatically the cheaper answer.

And the number you actually pay is a different number. Dealers in this industry mostly do not publish prices: they publish a configurator and a phone number. Expect the delivered price with a loader, a mower deck and dealer prep to land well above the list figure.

Before you sign anything

Work out the monthly payment before shortlisting machines, not after. It reorders the list: the difference between an L2502 and an LX2620 is about $3,000 of list price, which is small spread over a term and large if you are paying cash.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

How many acres do I need before a compact tractor makes sense?

There is no acreage answer, and anyone who gives you one is guessing. A quarter-acre with a gravel drive to plough and a trailer to move needs a loader; twenty acres of standing hay that you pay a neighbor to cut needs nothing. Choose by the heaviest recurring job, not the size of the deed.

Why do so many models stop at 23–25 horsepower?

Because of an emissions threshold, not engineering. US EPA nonroad rules split at 19 kW — 25 hp. Below that line the particulate limit is 0.4 g/kWh; at or above it, 0.03 g/kWh, thirteen times stricter. Manufacturers keep whole model families just under the line, which is why the class clusters at 23.3 and 24.8 hp.

Sub-compact or compact?

Compare the hitch, not the badge. A Kubota BX1880 lifts 680 lb at the three-point; an LX2620 with almost the same 23.3 hp engine lifts 1676 lb and moves 8.7 gallons a minute of hydraulic flow against 6.2. The engines are similar, the machines are not.

Is the manufacturer's price what I will pay?

No, and not only because of tax. MSRP excludes dealer fees, delivery and optional equipment, and it is quoted for the base configuration — usually without the loader that made you want the tractor. Dealers also rarely publish prices at all — the industry sells through a quote, which is why every dealer site has a "build your package" form instead of a cart.

What is the best compact tractor for the money?

There is no single best compact tractor, and any page that names one is selling something. On published figures the value point in the current Kubota range is the L2502 at $17,449 — it undercuts the B2601 sub-compact by $2,568 while lifting more at the hitch. If hydraulics matter, the LX2620 moves 8.7 GPM against 5.2 for $2,888 more, and a Kioti CK2620 moves 11.73. Best for the money depends entirely on which of those numbers your job list actually needs.

What is the best sub compact tractor?

In the Kubota range the BX1880 is the cheapest way in at $12,798 with 13.7 hp at the PTO, and the B2601 is the heavier build at $20,017 with 19.5 hp and a stronger loader. Across brands the John Deere 1025R has the widest dealer network, which matters more over ten years of ownership than a horsepower either way.

Do I need four-wheel drive?

On anything with a slope, mud or a loader, yes. A loaded bucket puts weight ahead of the front axle and takes it off the rear, which is exactly when two-wheel drive stops pulling. Nearly every machine in this class is 4WD as standard for that reason.
