The B2601 is the machine that breaks the rule that a bigger tractor lifts more. It is a sub-compact by frame and footprint, and it puts 1,411 lb on a Category I hitch — within 22 lb of the compact L2502, and more than double what a John Deere 1025R will lift.

It also costs $2,568 more than that L2502, which is the part that needs explaining.

What this machine is for

Loader work and rear implements on a property where a compact will not fit or is not wanted. Three-range hydrostatic drive, a mid PTO at 2,500 rpm for a mower deck, and 8.3 GPM of pump capacity — this is the sub-compact configured for work rather than for groundskeeping.

The step up from a BX is real: same class of engine as the BX2680, more than double the hitch capacity.

Why it is priced above the bigger machine

MSRP in this class describes a base configuration, not a size. Kubota's own lineup is not monotonic: the B2301 lists above the B2401 despite less power, and the L4802 lists below the L3902. Each price belongs to whatever that particular model ships with as standard.

For a buyer the practical consequence is simple. Do not shortlist by price, and do not assume the more expensive machine is the more capable one. Compare hitch capacity, PTO horsepower and flow, then look at what each one lists for.

Where it sits against the alternatives

Against the L2502: 22 lb less lift, 0.3 hp less at the PTO, 3.1 GPM more total flow, a mid PTO the L2502 does not offer, and $2,568 more on the sticker. Against the John Deere 1025R, the natural cross-brand rival, it lifts 1,411 lb to Deere's 681 and delivers 19.5 PTO hp to Deere's 18 — a different machine at a similar price point.

Against Kubota's own BX2680: same engine class, 731 lb more lift, $2,385 more money.

What we could not verify

Kubota publishes the full specification column only for the first model of each series, so hitch capacity, flow and weight here come from TractorData rather than from the manufacturer. The loader lift figure is not confirmed for this model at all — the 948 lb often quoted belongs to the B2301 spec sheet, so we leave it out.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Why does the B2601 cost more than the larger L2502?

Because MSRP in this class tracks the base configuration, not the size of the machine. The B2601 lists at $20,017 against $17,449 for an L2502 — $2,568 more for a smaller tractor that lifts 22 lb more at the hitch. What the money buys is a machine that fits where the L2502 does not, plus a mid PTO the L2502 has no equivalent of.

What are the B2601 specifications?

24.3 hp at the engine and 19.5 hp at the PTO, a Category I hitch lifting 1,411 lb at 24 inches behind it (1,808 lb at the lift ends), 8.3 GPM of total hydraulic flow, a Kubota 1.1L three-cylinder diesel, three-range hydrostatic drive and 1,632 lb of machine.

Is the B2601 a sub-compact or a compact?

Kubota sells it as a sub-compact and TractorData files it as a compact utility tractor, which tells you where it sits — the frame and footprint are sub-compact, the hitch is not. At 1,411 lb of lift it is within 22 lb of the compact L2502 and more than double a 1025R.

What can it run on the PTO?

19.5 hp at the shaft covers a four-foot rotary cutter, a light rotary tiller worked slowly, a snowblower and a mid-mount deck from the 2,500 rpm mid PTO. It does not cover a five-foot tiller in heavy ground — that is a machine-and-a-half job in this class regardless of brand.

Does the horsepower figure differ between sources?

Yes, and we publish the manufacturer's. Kubota states 24.3 hp; TractorData lists 25.5 hp gross for the same model. Measurement standards differ, so we take the figure from the company that builds it and note the other one rather than quietly picking the bigger number.
