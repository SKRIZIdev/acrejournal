The BX2680 is the most engine Kubota will sell you on the BX frame. At 23.3 hp it has half again the power of a BX1880 for $4,834 more, and it keeps everything else about the smaller machine — including the 680 lb hitch.

That combination is the whole story of this tractor: plenty of power at the shaft, not much capacity behind it.

What this machine is for

Groundskeeping at scale. A mid-mount deck driven from the 2,500 rpm mid PTO, a loader, a snowblower, a rear blade. It does that work faster and on steeper ground than a BX1880 because of the engine, and it fits in the same shed.

Where it stops is anything you drag through soil. 680 lb is the ceiling, and it does not move.

The hitch is the constraint, not the engine

19.5 hp at the PTO is more than a John Deere 2025R delivers, and that machine lifts 1,150 lb. Here the numbers point the other way: the shaft could drive a wider implement than the hitch can carry. So the useful question for this tractor is never "how much power" but "how heavy is the implement".

Two machines to compare it against on exactly that point: the B2601, same engine class, 1,411 lb of lift, $2,385 more; and the L2502, a proper compact at 1,389 lb of lift for $183 less than this BX. If ground engagement is on your list, the BX is the expensive way to find that out.

Where it sits against the alternatives

Against the BX1880 — the same tractor with more engine, and worth it if you mow acreage or push snow uphill. Against the John Deere 1025R — 680 lb of hitch against 681, 19.5 PTO hp against 18, and a published price against a quote. Against the B2601 or the L2502 — a different job entirely.

What we could not verify

Kubota publishes the full specification column only for the BX1880, so the hitch, flow, PTO and weight figures here come from TractorData. The loader lift capacity is not published for this model by either source, so this page does not state one. The two sources also disagree on engine power — 23.3 hp from Kubota, 24.8 hp gross from TractorData — and we print the manufacturer's figure with the difference noted.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

What is the difference between a BX1880 and a BX2680?

The engine, and almost nothing else that matters structurally. Both sit on the same frame with the same 680 lb hitch and the same 6.2 GPM pump. The BX1880 has 16.6 hp and 13.7 at the PTO; the BX2680 has 23.3 hp and 19.5 at the PTO, for $4,834 more. You are buying power, not capacity.

What are the BX2680 specifications?

23.3 hp at the engine per Kubota, 19.5 hp at the PTO per TractorData, a Category I hitch lifting 680 lb at 24 inches behind it, 6.2 GPM of implement flow, 1,521 lb of machine, hydrostatic drive, rear PTO at 540 rpm and mid PTO at 2,500 rpm. MSRP from $17,632.

Is 680 lb of lift enough?

For a blade, a light box scraper, a snowblower and a mid-mount deck, yes. For a rotary cutter or a tiller, no — most in this size weigh more than the hitch is rated to lift before they touch the ground. Buyers who need ground engagement should be looking at a B2601 or an L2502, which lift roughly twice as much.

Why does a BX2680 have more PTO power than the hitch can use?

Because the two limits are independent. 19.5 hp at the shaft would drive a wider implement than 680 lb of hitch will carry, so on this machine the hitch is the binding constraint. It is the opposite of the John Deere 2025R, where a stronger hitch is limited by 18 PTO hp.

Does it take a loader?

Yes, and it is the usual configuration. Kubota does not publish the loader lift figure for this model — only for the first model of the series — so we do not quote one.
