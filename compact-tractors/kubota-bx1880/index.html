The BX1880 is where the Kubota range starts, at $12,798 for the base machine. It is a real tractor — Category I hitch, live PTO, four-wheel drive, diesel — at a price closer to a garden tractor than to a compact.

It is also the least powerful machine Kubota sells, at 16.6 hp and 13.7 hp at the PTO, and that is the figure to think about rather than the price.

What this machine is for

Mowing one to three acres from a mid-mount deck, clearing a driveway, moving mulch and soil in a small loader bucket. Within that, it is well matched: 6.2 GPM of flow is generous for the size, and the loader lifts 739 lb — more than the three-point hitch will.

Where it runs out is width and speed. Every job in this class is deck width times speed, and this machine is short of both compared with the rest of the range.

The quick attach costs you 126 lb

Kubota publishes two loader ratings for this machine: 739 lb with a rigid mount and 613 lb with the quick attach. That is a 17% reduction, and it is the kind of figure that only shows up in the full specification column — brochures print the larger one.

It is not a reason to skip the quick attach. Swapping a bucket for forks in thirty seconds is worth more to most owners than 126 lb of lift they rarely use. But if you are at the limit, know which number you are working to.

Where it sits against the alternatives

Against the BX2680: identical frame, hitch and pump; 6.7 hp more engine and 5.8 hp more at the PTO for $4,834. Against the L2502, the cheapest compact: $4,651 more buys double the hitch capacity (1,389 lb) and 6.1 hp more at the PTO, but a bigger machine with no mid-mount deck.

The honest framing is that this is not a small compact — it is a well-built groundskeeping machine. Buyers who want a tractor for land work and choose this on price replace it, and that is the most expensive route through this class.

What we could not verify

Nothing on this page is second-hand: it is the one model in the BX series for which Kubota publishes the complete specification column, and every figure in the table comes from there. Weight is the exception — 1,336 lb is TractorData's figure, since Kubota's series page does not list it.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

What is the cheapest new Kubota tractor?

The BX1880, at $12,798 MSRP for the base configuration. That is the entry point to the whole lineup and roughly $4,650 below the L2502, which is the cheapest compact. Tax, dealer fees, delivery, the loader and the mower deck are all extra, and in this class the attachments can approach the price of the tractor.

What are the BX1880 specifications?

16.6 hp at the engine and 13.7 hp at the PTO, a Category I hitch lifting 680 lb at 24 inches behind it, a loader rated 739 lb rigid or 613 lb with the quick attach, 6.2 GPM of implement flow, two-range hydrostatic drive, ROPS, four-wheel drive and 1,336 lb of machine.

Is 13.7 hp at the PTO enough for a mower deck?

For a 48 to 60 inch mid-mount deck on residential grass, yes — that is what the machine is built around, driven from the 2,500 rpm mid PTO. For a rear rotary cutter in rough growth it is thin, and for a tiller it is below what most are specified to need.

How much land does a BX1880 suit?

Realistically one to three acres of grass with a driveway to clear. Beyond that the limit is not capability but time — mowing time is deck width times speed, and this is the slowest machine in the range. Owners with five acres and this tractor usually say they would buy more engine next time.

BX1880 or BX2680?

Same frame, same 680 lb hitch, same 6.2 GPM pump. The BX2680 has 23.3 hp against 16.6 and 19.5 at the PTO against 13.7, for $4,834 more. If the deck is the main tool and the ground is flat, the BX1880 is enough. If you push snow uphill or mow acreage, the extra engine is what you are actually short of.
