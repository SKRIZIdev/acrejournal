The 2025R is the answer to a question the 1025R raises. Both have the same engine class and the same 18 hp at the PTO, so on a spec sheet they look like the same tractor at two prices. They are not: the 2025R lifts 1,150 lb where the 1025R lifts 681, and carries 216 lb more of its own weight.

What this machine is for

Everything a 1025R does, plus implements that go into the ground. The hitch is the reason to be here. A box blade, a middle buster, a narrow tiller, a rear blade with some weight to it — all of these are within 1,150 lb, and none of them are within 681.

What it does not fix is the PTO. 18 hp is 18 hp, and the implement width that rating allows does not change because the hitch got stronger. Buyers who read the lift figure as "now I can run a five-foot tiller" are reading the wrong number.

Two lift figures, and why the smaller one is the honest one

TractorData publishes both: 1,433 lb at the ends of the lift arms and 1,150 lb at 24 inches behind the hitch. Both are true. The difference is leverage — an implement's mass sits behind the hitch, not on the arm ends, so the 24 inch figure is the one that describes real work. Manufacturer brochures across this class tend to print whichever number is larger, which is why comparing two machines from two brochures is unreliable.

Our tables use the 24 inch figure everywhere, for every brand, so the comparison holds.

John Deere 2025R price, as far as it is on record

No current list price reaches us, and we are not going to publish an estimate as though it were one. What is on record from TractorData is the original list: $14,434 in 2013 and $15,348 in 2014. Nothing since.

For comparison, the 1025R below it in the range is recorded at $18,013 for 2026 — so a current 2025R quote sits above that, and how far above depends entirely on the package. Ask the dealer to price the tractor, the loader and the deck as separate lines; in this class the attachments carry the margin.

Where it sits against the alternatives

Against the 1025R: more hitch, more weight, same PTO. Against Kubota's B2601 — the machine this competes with in showrooms — the Deere lifts 1,150 lb to the Kubota's 1,411 lb, and the Kubota moves 8.3 GPM total to the Deere's 5.6. On paper the B2601 wins the hydraulics and the hitch; the Deere answers with dealer coverage.

Against a proper compact like the L2502, the 2025R is still a sub-compact: 1,150 lb against 1,389 lb of lift, and 3.4 GPM against 5.2 to the implement.

What we could not verify

The loader lift capacity: TractorData lists a front-end loader as an option without publishing a figure, and Deere's site does not serve us. There is no current list price either — the 2014 figure in the table is the last one recorded, and we label it as history rather than dressing it up as a quote.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

What is the difference between a 1025R and a 2025R?

The engine and the PTO rating are effectively the same — 24.2 hp against 23.8, and 18 hp at the PTO on both. The difference is the hitch and the mass. The 2025R lifts 1,150 lb at 24 inches behind the hitch against 681 lb, and it weighs 1,660 lb against 1,444. If your implement list is a mower deck and a blade, the extra buys you nothing. If it includes anything that goes into the ground, it is the whole point.

What are the 2025R specifications?

24.2 hp engine, 18 hp at the PTO, Category I hitch lifting 1,150 lb at 24 inches (1,433 lb measured at the lift ends), 3.4 GPM of implement flow out of 5.6 GPM total, 1,660 lb of machine on hydrostatic drive, rear PTO at 540 rpm and mid PTO at 2,000.

How much does a 2025R cost?

We do not have a current figure and will not invent one. TractorData's most recent original price for this model is $15,348 for 2014; Deere does not publish a list price to us, and dealers quote a package rather than a machine. Treat the 2014 number as history, not as a target.

Can it run a rotary tiller?

The hitch can hold one, the PTO is the question. 18 hp at the PTO is at the bottom of what a four to five foot tiller is specified to need, and tillers are a continuous load, not a peak one. A narrower tiller worked slowly is realistic; a five-foot tiller in heavy soil is not.

Is a mid-mount mower deck available?

Yes — 54 and 62 inch decks are on the factory list, along with a front-end loader, a backhoe, a blade and a snowblower. The mid PTO runs at 2,000 rpm, which is what the deck is driven from.
