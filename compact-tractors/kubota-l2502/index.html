The L2502 is the machine most people mean when they say "compact tractor" without knowing it. It sits at the point where the class stops being a large mower and starts being a tractor — a Category I hitch that lifts 1,389 lb, a live PTO, and a frame that will take a loader without complaint.

It is also the cheapest way into that capability from a major brand. At $17,449 list it undercuts Kubota's own B2601 sub-compact by $2,500 while lifting more.

What this machine is for

Loader work, land clearing, moving material, running a rotary cutter or a tiller on a few acres. It is a working machine rather than a groundskeeping one: there is no mid-mount mower deck in the usual configuration, and if mowing a lawn is the main job, this is the wrong tractor.

Where it stops being enough is continuous hydraulic work and heavy tillage. At 5.2 GPM the pump is sized for a loader and a hitch, not for an attachment with a hydraulic motor running all day. And 19.8 hp at the PTO sets a hard ceiling on implement width — a six-foot tiller in heavy soil will find the limit quickly.

The transmission decision

This is the one choice on the order form that you cannot change later, and the horsepower figures point the wrong way if you read them alone.

Gear drive gives you 19.8 hp at the PTO against 18.4 for hydrostatic. On paper, gear wins. In practice the question is how often you change direction. Loader work is a cycle of forward, stop, reverse, stop, repeated hundreds of times an hour; doing that on a clutch and a shift lever is exhausting. Field work with an implement is the opposite — one direction, one speed, for hours.

The rule owners repeat, and it holds up: if the loader is the reason you are buying the tractor, take the hydrostatic and accept the 1.4 hp. If the tractor is for the field, take the gears.

Where it sits against the alternatives

Against Kubota's own range, the L2502 is the value point. The LX2620 has the same 23.3 hp engine but a stronger hitch (1,676 lb) and considerably more hydraulic flow (8.7 GPM) for about $2,900 more. If your implement list is growing, that is money well spent; if it is a loader and a cutter, it is not.

The B2601 sub-compact costs more and lifts less, and makes sense only if the machine has to fit somewhere the L2502 will not.

The direct cross-brand comparison people ask about is the John Deere 3025E. We have not verified Deere's current specification ourselves — their site blocks our checks — so the comparison page carries manufacturer figures we could not confirm, and says so.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Why is this model 23.3 horsepower and not 26?

To stay under the US EPA nonroad threshold at 19 kW, which is 25 hp. Below that line the particulate limit is 0.4 g/kWh; at or above it the limit is 0.03 g/kWh, thirteen times stricter, and the machine needs aftertreatment hardware to meet it. Kubota keeps the whole L02 entry model under the line, which is why the next step up in the family jumps straight to 33 hp.

What does the L2502 replace?

The L2501. The L02 generation superseded the L01 range, and dealers were running clearance offers on L02 stock through the 2026 season. If you are reading a review or a forum thread about an L2501, the specifications are close but not identical, and the parts numbers are not interchangeable across the board.

DT or HST — which transmission?

The gear version (DT, 8 forward and 8 reverse) gives 19.8 hp at the PTO; the hydrostatic gives 18.4. Gear drive is the choice for steady work in a field, where you set a speed and stay there. HST wins wherever you change direction constantly — loader cycles, yard work, snow — at the cost of about 1.4 hp and a slightly higher price.

Is 5.2 GPM of hydraulic flow enough?

For a loader and a rear implement, yes. For anything with a hydraulic motor running continuously — a grapple used hard, a hydraulic-drive attachment — it is on the low side. An LX2620 moves 8.7 GPM for comparison, and that difference is more noticeable in daily use than the horsepower figures suggest.

What does it actually cost to put one in the yard?

The $17,449 list price is the base machine, before tax, dealer fees, delivery and the loader. Dealers in this class rarely publish a delivered price at all — they publish a configurator. Budget well above list once a loader and a mid-mount deck are on it.
