These rules are not decoration — the site is built so that breaking them is visible. A figure without a source is flagged when the page is built, and a page with no facts table is flagged too.

Acre Journal covers compact tractors, used equipment, irrigation and greenhouses for people who work a few acres rather than a few thousand. The reason it exists is narrow: almost everything written about this equipment is either a dealer brochure retyped, or a page assembled to rank and never checked by anyone who owns the machine.

What we publish

Four kinds of page, and they are built differently on purpose.

Owner reports aggregate documented owner experience about one machine — what breaks, what holds, what owners would buy again. Every report states its sample size, the period it covers, and a section listing what we could not verify. It is the part of the site that cannot be copied, because it is work rather than rewriting.

Learn pages answer one question each and are written to be quotable: a short answer at the top, a facts table with sources, and the explanation below it.

Comparisons put two machines side by side on published specifications, plus owner-reported behavior where it contradicts the brochure. We do not score machines out of ten — a single number hides the trade-off that actually decides the purchase.

Tools are calculators that run in your browser. Each one lists what it assumes, and those assumptions are on the page rather than buried in the code. The irrigation calculator queries the USDA soil survey for your ZIP rather than guessing your soil.

How the numbers are checked

Every figure in a facts table carries a source, and the site build prints a warning for any row that does not have one. That is a mechanical check, not a promise: it means an unsourced number cannot quietly reach the published page, because it shows up every time the site is rebuilt.

Where a row describes our own method rather than an external measurement, the source points here, to this page. Where a figure comes from a university extension service, a federal dataset or a manufacturer specification, the row links to it directly.

What we will not do

We will not write first-person testing copy for equipment nobody here has operated. Beyond being dishonest, it is illegal in the United States: the FTC rule on fake reviews carries penalties per violation, and a review update from Google removes exactly this kind of page.

We will not publish location pages for towns we have no presence in, and we will not generate model pages in bulk from a spreadsheet. Pages here exist because there is something specific to say on them.

How we make money

Affiliate commissions on equipment and supplies, and referral fees on equipment financing. Those links are marked, and outside the calculator pages there are never more than three commercial exits on a page — a limit the build enforces rather than a preference we state.

Advertising does not buy a verdict, a shortlist position or a rating. If that changes, this page changes first.

Who writes here

The editorial standard above is the part that does not change with the byline. The named author, their background and how to reach them go here before launch.

Corrections

If a figure here is wrong, it gets fixed and the page says what changed. Reports and comparisons carry an updated date, and a correction that changes a verdict is stated in the text rather than edited away silently.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

Do you test the machines you write about?

Not all of them, and we say which. Where we have hands-on experience we write in the first person and describe the conditions. Where we do not, the page is built from owner reports and published specifications, and it says so at the top.

Where do the owner reports come from?

Public owner threads, forum histories and direct interviews with owners and technicians. Each report states how many owners it covers, what period, and what we could not verify.

How does Acre Journal make money?

Affiliate links and, on financing pages, referral fees. Links that pay us are marked. We do not sell placement inside a verdict or a shortlist.

Why are some figures marked as pending a source?

Because they are not sourced yet. Marking them is deliberate: the alternative is a confident number nobody can check, which is exactly what this niche is already full of.
